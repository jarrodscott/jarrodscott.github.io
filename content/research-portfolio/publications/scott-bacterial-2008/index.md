abstract: Host-microbe symbioses play a critical role in the evolution of biological
  diversity and complexity. In a notably intricate system, southern pine beetles  use
  symbiotic fungi to help overcome host-tree defenses and to provide nutrition  for
  their larvae. We show that this beetle-fungal mutualism is chemically  mediated
  by a bacterially produced polyunsaturated peroxide. The molecule's  selective toxicity
  toward the beetle's fungal antagonist, combined with the  prevalence and localization
  of its bacterial source, indicates an insect-microbe  association that is both mutualistic
  and coevolved. This unexpected finding in a  well-studied system indicates that
  mutualistic associations between insects and  antibiotic-producing bacteria are
  more common than currently recognized and that  identifying their small-molecule
  mediators can provide a powerful search strategy  for therapeutically useful antimicrobial
  compounds.
